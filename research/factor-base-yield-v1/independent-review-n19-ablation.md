**Independent n=19 same-dimension ablation addendum — verdict: CONCUR.**

I concur with the narrowly stated, descriptive implementation claim in the [ablation review plan](/Volumes/SSD990/wdsat-binary-sat-review-20260909/research/factor-base-yield-v1/review-plan-n19-ablation.json). At fixed `n=19`, `k=6`, Frobenius schedule `[0,1,2]`, Gaussian WDSat binary, exact membership model and stopping rule, the frozen sparse basis used fewer targets and less solver and recorded per-target pipeline time than the frozen trace basis on each of three distinct public `Q` instances. The independently recomputed medians are **90 versus 44 targets**, **92.995857 versus 37.118965 seconds of solver subprocess time**, and **110.961086 versus 47.775270 seconds of recorded per-target pipeline time**. The corresponding ratios of medians are **2.045455×**, **2.505346×**, and **2.322563×**.

This addendum resolves the specified dimension, schedule and runtime full-log-oracle objections for this experiment. It does not remove the remaining distinction between a selected basis and density as an isolated causal variable, or establish whole-program performance, general scaling, novelty, or superiority to Pollard rho. The earlier qualified verdict for the differently configured, mixed-timing comparison remains unchanged.

**Conditions held fixed and intended differences.** Both arms use the same curve `y²+xy=x³+x²+1`, field modulus `z^19+z^5+z^2+z+1`, subgroup order `r=262543`, generator `P=(393246,31114)`, affine offset 1, three factors of dimension 6, schedule `[0,1,2]`, and 37 unary Boolean variables. Both use the same native binary with `-x`, chained S3 representation, variable layout, exact membership construction, sign recovery, Frobenius rewrite, rank routine, 30-second per-call watchdog, 128-target ceiling, and first verified useful-dependency stopping criterion. There is no reused initial prefix.

Within each stream the target sequence is identical between arms, and active-arm execution order is shuffled using a separate deterministic RNG. The changed input is the direction basis: `[2,4,8,16,32,64]` versus `[8,16,128,1024,4096,32768]`. Differences in allowed points, exact membership clauses, expanded equations, geometric sumsets, solver search and rank behavior follow from that basis choice. They are legitimate parts of this basis ablation, but the experiment does not vary density independently while holding those properties fixed.

**No-log-oracle audit.** The new eigenvalue function counts the two points on the curve over `F₂`, so its Frobenius trace is `t=1`. It obtains the roots of `T²−T+2` modulo `r`, namely 84,450 and 178,094, and selects 84,450 by checking `π(P)=[λ]P`. The reviewer independently obtained the same unique matching root using a polynomial-root calculation and public point equality. No subgroup walk or discrete-log lookup is required for this construction.

The runtime driver contains no full subgroup-log table. Its coverage validator enumerates actual factor points and their point sums, independently of the secrets and target streams. The SAT adapter receives the curve, factor spaces, `R`, subgroup order and solver parameters; the rank accumulator receives only coefficients, `a`, `b` and the modulus. The stream secret is used to generate the test instance `Q` and to check the already derived answer. Target generation uses `aP+bQ`; reachability is tested after the solver call and does not choose whether a target is submitted. I found no secret or log-table input to either the decomposition solver or the matrix extraction.

A provenance qualification remains: the driver reads its preselected basis and historical `selection_seconds` from the old frontier artifact. That old frontier constructor did build a log dictionary, though the density-selection objective queried only x-membership keys and used no scalar values. I therefore reproduced selection independently using only half-trace point lifting and actual subgroup tests. Across **18,564 candidate six-direction bases** and **31,180 distinct x inputs**, the same density winner and tie-break were obtained: exponents `[3,4,7,10,12,15]`, basis `[8,16,128,1024,4096,32768]`, with 45 valid assignments. This establishes that the selected basis is reproducible without a log oracle; it does not convert the historical 0.090776-second selection-loop timing into a newly measured complete oracle-free setup cost.

**What is resolved, and what remains.** Dimension and schedule confounding are resolved: both are identical and checked at the raw-formula level. Runtime full-log-oracle dependence is resolved: λ is algebraic, coverage is computed from public points, and decomposition/rank inputs contain no log table or secret. Historical basis selection is also independently reproducible with membership alone. Solver provenance is strengthened by a deterministic independent rebuild. Three distinct public `Q` values resolve the earlier same-instance restriction within this one curve.

The experiment still compares one selected sparse basis with one fixed trace basis on one field representation, curve and machine. It does not isolate density from point-addition structure, monomial directions, clause shape, rank dependence or solver order; those all change with the chosen basis. The first member of that causal chain—the basis—is the controlled intervention, and “basis-yield ablation” is a defensible description. A general assertion that maximizing density always optimizes extraction would require a panel of bases, including equal-density controls, and separate held-out curves/seeds. Claims of a population or scaling speedup also need substantially more repeated measurements and fixed uncertainty/reporting rules. These are conditions for stronger future claims, not failures of the bounded numerical comparison audited here.

The prior-art boundaries remain those of the earlier review. Affine factor spaces and SAT experiments appear in Galbraith and Gebregiyorgis, [Summation polynomial algorithms for elliptic curves in characteristic two](https://eprint.iacr.org/2014/806.pdf), Section 6; WDSat and representation-specific SAT techniques appear in Trimoska, Ionica and Dequen, [A SAT-Based Approach for Index Calculus on Binary Elliptic Curves](https://eprint.iacr.org/2019/313.pdf); Frobenius-aware relation rewriting is developed in Galbraith, Granger, Merz and Petit, [On index calculus algorithms for subfield curves](https://sacworkshop.org/SAC20/files/preproceedings/18-IndexCalculus.pdf). None of this establishes or disproves novelty of this exact sparse-basis combination. It also supplies no warrant to extrapolate three toy extractions to standardized parameters, an asymptotic ECDLP improvement, or an advantage over automorphism-aware Pollard rho.

**Narrowest defensible claim.** On the specified n=19 Koblitz curve, after fixing k=6, schedule `[0,1,2]`, solver binary and protocol, replacing the fixed trace basis with the selected sparse trace-affine basis increased exact point-decomposition coverage from 30.486% to 93.600%. Across the three archived distinct `Q` instances, both configurations produced independently verified relation-dependency extraction; the selected basis used fewer attempts and less recorded time in every pair. Median attempts were 90 versus 44; the ratios of the solver-only and per-target pipeline medians were 2.505346× and 2.322563×. This is a reproducible bounded implementation result for this basis intervention, with complete program/setup cost and general cryptanalytic consequences outside the evidence.
